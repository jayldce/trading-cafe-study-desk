### Breakout candle vs follow-up candle
**What:** the first candle that closes above a level is the *breakout candle*. The next candle, if it holds above, is the *follow-up*.
**Why it matters:** breakout candles often trap buyers. Price pokes above, stops trigger, then it reverses. Waiting for the follow-up filters out many false breakouts, at the cost of a slightly worse price.
**His words:** to avoid false breakouts, watch the follow-up zone patiently.
**Crowded trade (18-Sep):** every retail trader sees the same breakout, so the trap is heavy; buying the break itself makes you "the liquidity that buys the top". Enter on the first pullback or the follow-up instead. [▶ 18-Sep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EPh5qxWEUhU&t=4020s)
**Midday needs a shakeout first (13-Oct):** the larger the consolidation, the more orders sit just beyond it, so he lets the break candle pass and wants two or three candles of halt, then a higher high and higher low. At midday he added that no breakout is trustworthy "until a shakeout has formed and come out". The 22700 PE version — candle close first, small pullback, entry above 157.57 with the breakout candle's low 141.9 as stop — hit its 207.99 target (+50.42). [▶ 10:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoHdfFTi3y0&t=3600s) · [▶ 10:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoHdfFTi3y0&t=4200s) · [▶ 12:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoHdfFTi3y0&t=11700s)
**Three in one session, and a double-confirmation variant (15-Oct, Sensex expiry):** "this is the follow-up candle — above 300" on the 74000 PE (+60.3), "above 300 if it sustains on the follow-up" on the 73800 CE (+59), and "above 286 on the follow-up, otherwise ignore it" on the 74500 PE (+97.9), all with stops of 5–25 option points. **Disqualified when** the consolidation is long (more candles, more false breaks), no follow-up forms ("ignore"), or one candle's stop alone is too big. Variant: when a one-candle breakout reverses, mark *that candle's high* and trade only above it with a small stop. ### "Small candles at one level → blast" / barcode pattern
**What:** a squeeze. When many small, similar candles hug the same level (he calls it a **barcode pattern**, or a "dangal" wrestling match between buyers and sellers), the two sides are balanced and energy is building. The break is usually fast.
**How he uses it:** don't trade *inside* the barcode, because the range keeps shrinking and you get chopped. Wait for one side to win, then take the follow-up candle after the break (playbook setup A).
**28-Oct, the cluster rule:** three or four candles hugging one level trap both sides inside, so entries there get stopped by whichever breakout comes; wait for the cluster to break, ideally after a small red candle so the stop is smaller. Back-to-back 2-point candles after a big move mean the next momentum comes from the break, and Sensex needs a 70–80-point move to pay a normal stop, so he does not scalp 15–20 inside [▶ 11:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wjb4_5XDOC8&t=7080s) · [▶ 10:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wjb4_5XDOC8&t=5160s). **27-Oct:** equal-length candles at a support-turned-resistance (the 22500 CE under 181) mark the make-or-break level; "until it clears, the CE sellers are still there" — the close above 181 at 14:31 ran to ~268 [▶ 14:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=az8Ezg6JWks&t=18900s).
**Watch:** [▶ 11-Sep 09:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTDwaNEZ2RY&t=1200s) · [▶ 8-Sep barcode explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s3r5EJy4cw8&t=3420s)
**Tall zigzag candles are the opposite (7-Oct):** a run of big alternating candles means fast moves, so he plans a small-risk entry in advance rather than chasing one. [▶ 11:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F-PdJ_DvsDY&t=9420s)
**Big candles with no follow-up are a stand-aside (22-Oct):** on the expiry open the third and fourth large candles each came without a follow-up, so every breakout or continuation needed an ~80-pt one-candle stop. He called it a barcode market and waited for a candle that showed strength with a pullback stop of the 25–30 pts he treats as normal on Sensex. [▶ 09:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AuH5dwLyyx8&t=1740s) · [▶ 09:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AuH5dwLyyx8&t=1980s)

### Make-or-break zone
**What:** a level where price was rejected several times (e.g. 176 on the CE, 162 on the PE). Whichever side breaks with an aggressive candle is the side to trade.
**Watch:** [▶ 09:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTDwaNEZ2RY&t=1260s)

### Scalper entry vs traditional entry
- **Scalper:** buy directly as price crosses the level. Tighter, faster, more false signals.
- **Traditional:** wait for a strong candle, then buy the pullback. Fewer trades, better SL.
- **Analyse on 5 minutes, execute on 1 minute (13-Oct):** he plots zones on the 5-minute (or 1-hour / 4-hour) chart and executes on a 1-minute candle close at that zone, because the 1-minute confirmation candle sits close to the zone and the stop comes out at 10–15 points instead of 15–30. Executing on the higher timeframe is allowed only with a cut in quantity. [▶ 11:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoHdfFTi3y0&t=9180s) · [▶ 11:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoHdfFTi3y0&t=9360s)
**Watch:** [▶ 09:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTDwaNEZ2RY&t=1380s)

### When is a pullback trustworthy?
**What:** not every dip after a move is a pullback you can buy. Most are not, and he says so out loud far more often than he takes one.

**Why it works:** a pullback is only tradeable when it returns to a zone that already *produced* a move — the base a breakout launched from, which he calls the **apex**. Buyers who were filled there once are willing again, and the people who chased the extended candle are now offside. A dip to nowhere in particular has neither.

**Qualifies when:**
- the move it is retracing was **aggressive**. A ~100-point spot move on 11-Sep was not enough, and he left it alone [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTDwaNEZ2RY&t=2280s). On 14-Oct he put it as "a ball thrown low doesn't bounce high": the morning rally had not gone far, the 73200 CE bounce off its base ran only +27 and came back to cost [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSIAdI3_Gu8&t=2940s).
- it returns to a zone he had **marked before** price got there — on 25-Aug the entry came just above a 268 base / ~286 breakout zone he had been watching for hours: "यह पुलबैक एंट्री मैं सुबह से ढूंढने की कोशिश कर रहा था" [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DmPpZPOBuNA&t=13800s).
- the resulting stop is **small**. The whole point is a cheaper entry than the breakout offered.
- 29-Oct, twice on Sensex expiry: after the sharp 12:4x fall he said wait for the 73900 PE to come back into 177–189 rather than chase (203.31 → SL 166.47, +98.08), and at 13:46 the 73500 CE on one good green candle in the buying area once Sensex held 73,800 (375.81 → SL 339.45, +150.64) — both from his journal [▶ 12:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLu3xXwwUCQ&t=13260s) · [▶ 13:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLu3xXwwUCQ&t=16980s).

**Disqualified when:**
- **the stop comes out too big.** On 23-Sep he refused a ~30-point stop against a 585 target and waited for a smaller-risk pullback instead, saying plainly he didn't like it [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-VHSEbmW5Y&t=16500s).
- **the candle isn't a pullback candle.** On 25-Aug he sat through several candles after the breakout held, rejecting more than one out loud as "not a pullback candle" before the right one came [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DmPpZPOBuNA&t=13140s).
- **no green candle forms at the zone.** On 9-Oct the 22400 PE came back toward its 145–147 base after the first leg, and he passed: "a pullback needs a candle to form; it hasn't" — re-entry only above the 147 base [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kn5B0LK1YM&t=960s).
- **it is the pullback of a breakout that just failed.** "Re-entry on a breakout trade is not taken in the pullback, unless the candle was very strong" (9-Oct, straight after the 22450 CE stop-out); the failed break has already shown no strength, so the dip buys the same trapped zone. Wait for a fresh higher high [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kn5B0LK1YM&t=3780s).
- **price is still running.** 15-Oct: he refused every call on a running candle where the stop came out at 55–70 points and waited for the dip to the breakout zone (440 on the 73400 CE) so one candle defined the stop — that one paid +90.4 on a 6-point stop [▶ 09:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvf-FA_DAE0&t=660s) · [▶ 10:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvf-FA_DAE0&t=3660s).
- **the only entry is the breakout out of a stuck cluster (29-Oct).** With 3–4 candles stuck under a level on the 73500 CE, the breakout and follow-up candles both needed 50–67-point stops; he passed ("with big stops I'll be out of the game") and wanted the pullback to ~300–320 with a ~30-point stop instead [▶ 10:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLu3xXwwUCQ&t=3720s) · [▶ 10:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLu3xXwwUCQ&t=4740s).
- **it is a pullback against the day's trend.** 15-Oct's 74200 PE, a green pin bar at a small FVG on a ~700-point up day, was stopped −20.3 within minutes — the same failure as 8-Oct's call pullback on a crash day [▶ 12:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvf-FA_DAE0&t=9420s).
- **the structure calls for a breakout entry instead.** On 1-Oct, approaching a 459–460 zone: "इसमें पुलबैक एंट्री नहीं देख रहा हूं। इसमें क्लियर ब्रेकआउट एंट्री देख रहा हूं" — and he accepted a 25–30 point stop, the top of his own ceiling, because the entry type was different [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jCedaeF9RWg&t=14640s).

- **it is the second visit.** "That is already the pullback; if it comes a second time, do not take it" (23-Oct, 22600 PE) — and at 13:20 a second bounce at the same put zone offered a smaller reward [▶ 11:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLVGnIzQs6U&t=7920s).
- **the third visit, said in so many words (28-Oct).** The first and second pullbacks to a breakout zone are entries; a third return means "the market does not want to go up" and he stops looking for the pullback [▶ 11:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wjb4_5XDOC8&t=7920s). **Trigger:** a small, calm candle **at** the marked zone — not the pullback candle itself. ### Liquidity sweep (stop hunt)
**What:** stop losses cluster just below obvious lows. Big players push price below the low to fill their buy orders against those stops, and then price reverses.
**How he trades it:** after the sweep, wait for a strong bullish close, enter on the first reversal sign, and keep the SL at the day low.
**Watch:** [▶ 10:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTDwaNEZ2RY&t=6240s) · frame `data/frames/qTDwaNEZ2RY/014340.jpg`
**The full recipe, three times on one expiry (19-Oct):** a zone marked beforehand on the option (163 on the 22850 CE, the 117–129 order block on the 23200 PE, ~116 on the 23000 PE), stops taken just beyond it, then a pin bar or green candle, and entry above that candle's high or on a close above the named level. Risk was 8–18 option points against 34–120 made (+120.54, +62.24, +34.41). **Disqualified when:** there is no previous level nearby ("mid-way, I don't see a previous level"), the reversal candle is missing, or price closes below the zone before the trigger. [▶ 09:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dDZg2d5JxLk&t=520s) · [▶ 10:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dDZg2d5JxLk&t=4080s) · [▶ 14:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dDZg2d5JxLk&t=20100s)
**Sweep into an option FVG, one candle of risk (22-Oct, Sensex expiry):** the 73100 CE dropped into a fair-value-gap zone it had left on the way up (~262–283), took the lows under it, and printed a good candle; he set the stop under that single candle (238.97) and entered above 279–280 on the follow-up, targeting the previous distribution zones (350, then ~452, then 562). It cleared 452 by 10:23. **Disqualified when** (same session, all named): the candle is still running ("chasing a running candle is meaningless"); big candles keep printing with no follow-up; the one-candle stop is 60–80 pts against ~60 of reward; or price only drifts slowly into the zone ("if it comes slowly, leave it") — he wants a pin bar that breaks the previous low and reverses, then the first green candle. [▶ 10:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AuH5dwLyyx8&t=3360s) · [▶ 10:13 recap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AuH5dwLyyx8&t=3780s) · [▶ 10:48 slow-drift rule](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AuH5dwLyyx8&t=5880s)
**Only a strong swing has stops worth sweeping (23-Oct):** at 14:34 he said the break of the 22450 CE's swing low did not count yet — that swing had been held by weak buyers, so there were no meaningful stops under it. He wanted price to reach the 160–170 zone where a 5-minute FVG and an order block sat, then a reversal candle, entry above 181 [▶ 14:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLVGnIzQs6U&t=19440s) · [▶ 14:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLVGnIzQs6U&t=19920s). The CE reached ~198.55 with no stated exit.
**Disqualified: no strength at the turn (29-Oct).** The put took the liquidity under the day low with a tiny stop on offer, and he declined: without a forceful candle away from the low "it will keep hitting again and again". He wanted a pin bar first, and named one on the put at 11:49 [▶ 11:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLu3xXwwUCQ&t=9960s). A small stop is what the sweep offers; strength is what qualifies it.

### Fair value gap (FVG) on the option chart
**What:** a zone price moved through too fast (an imbalance), left behind by a quick push to a fresh high or low. Price often returns to it.
**His use:** if price falls back into the FVG, the move gets "dull" and late traders are trapped. You want an aggressive candle to clear it.
**Watch:** [▶ 10:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTDwaNEZ2RY&t=6780s)
**7-Oct:** a viewer called a spot FVG mid-move; he said no — on his reading an FVG forms at a bottom after a strong move and a retest, and that move had not made one yet. [▶ 10:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F-PdJ_DvsDY&t=4560s)
**22-Oct:** the FVG he traded sat *at the lows* of the 73100 CE, exactly where his 7-Oct definition puts one (a bottom after a strong move, then a retest); the sweep of the lows into it was the entry. [▶ 10:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AuH5dwLyyx8&t=3780s)
**23-Oct, what disqualifies one:** a gap is only valid on the **first** tick back into it — "after that it is not valid for us" — and only after a strong move judged by its candles, not a slow grind [▶ 11:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLVGnIzQs6U&t=7560s) · [▶ 11:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLVGnIzQs6U&t=8040s). A "gap" whose high did not go above the previous high is not an FVG at all [▶ 12:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLVGnIzQs6U&t=11640s), and a very large gap is skipped because the stop under it is too wide for the reward. Trigger: a green candle at the first retest, entry above its high.

### Bounce strength
After price falls from a level it usually bounces. If each bounce is **weaker**, expect a breakdown from that same zone. A strong bounce means buyers are defending. After two bounces, a **third test** rarely holds, so don't buy it; wait for the break.
**Related, the dead-cat bounce:** a small reversal candle inside a strong move that fails to hold. The first green candle alone doesn't make a reversal; check its strength.
**28-Sep, traded on purpose:** he named a ~124 base on a falling 22850 CE a technical / dead-cat bounce, not a reversal, and still took it with a ~4-pt stop and a capped 137 target. It never got past ~134 and the stop went (about −4). Naming it a dead-cat bounce is what kept the stop and target small.
**Disqualified: the second visit to a zone that already paid (13-Oct).** He took the first bounce of the 22400 CE at ~125 (+21.15) and refused the re-entry at the same zone at 10:50, and at 11:14–11:18 refused calls at a 22,600 resistance tested again and again — "ये टूट जाएगा" (this will break). Each touch uses up the orders resting there; re-entry only after a strong candle through the old level, on the higher high. [▶ 10:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoHdfFTi3y0&t=6120s) · [▶ 11:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoHdfFTi3y0&t=7800s)
**21-Oct, the third visit in his words, and in the log:** at 11:07 he called it a ball bounce — each return to a zone bounces lower — and stood aside after two attempts [▶ 11:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TDWbe0WQjgo&t=7020s). ### Call writers at round strikes
Option sellers cluster at round strikes (23,300). When spot pushes through and holds, those writers cover ("give up"), which fuels the move. That's why round numbers make good targets and breakout levels.
**Real numbers (8-Sep):** 23,700 call OI fell from ~3.79 Cr to ~3.20 Cr as spot pushed through; that's writers covering. The chain that day also showed PCR 0.56 and max pain 23,800.
**Watch:** [▶ 11-Sep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTDwaNEZ2RY&t=6060s) · [▶ 8-Sep OI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s3r5EJy4cw8&t=13500s)

### Trapped writers: why a move accelerates past a level
**What:** his highest-conviction entries are not "the level broke" — they are "the people positioned against this break now have to buy it back". The move that follows is fast because it is forced, not voluntary.

**Why it works:** in an index option chain the trapped party has a name and an address. Writers sell calls at a strike expecting price to stay below it; when spot pushes through and *holds*, their short is losing and they cover by buying — which pushes price further through, which traps the next strike up. That is the fuel. On 8-Sep the 23,700 call OI fell from ~3.79 Cr to ~3.20 Cr as spot pushed through: that number is the covering, visible after the fact [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s3r5EJy4cw8&t=13500s). He calls the moment the writers "give up".

**Qualifies when:**
- there is a **real concentration** to trap — a strike with heavy open interest, which is why round numbers work so well as breakout levels.
- price **breaks and holds**, not just pokes. A wick through traps nobody; the writers only cover once the break sustains.
- the level is one price has **already been rejected from several times** — a make-or-break zone. Each rejection adds another layer of sellers who are now offside when it finally goes.
- **a second, lower high before the break** deepens it: that shape traps the buyers who bought the first high as well, and he flags it as fuelling a bigger counter-move [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jCedaeF9RWg&t=4380s).

**Disqualified when:**
- **the break has no one on the other side.** A level with no OI behind it produces a move with no forced buying, which is the ordinary grind rather than the blast.
- **the panic is yours.** His own worst sequences are the ones where he was the trapped party — three stopped attempts on the same PE before the real move, "frankly trapped" in his own words, and afterwards: "ट्रैप वाज़ वर्थ फॉर दिस काइंड ऑफ़ मूव" [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DmPpZPOBuNA&t=12240s). The same mechanism that pays you when you read it right is what takes the stop when you don't.
- **it is news or results doing the moving.** "रादर देन रिजल्ट वाले इसमें हाथ डाले, हम ऐसे ट्रेड्स देखेंगे जहां पे ढंग से समझ में आता है क्या हो रहा है" — only trade where the price action is legible [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ayYEP_dKaLY&t=17880s).

**Trigger:** the break that *holds*, entered the normal way — follow-up candle above the level, stop at that candle's low. The trapped-writer read raises conviction and justifies a wider target; it does not replace the entry rule.

**Honest caveat:** this mechanism is well documented *after* a move, and the OI change proves it happened. It does **not** tell you when. Tested over 8 logged sessions, neither PCR, IV skew nor distance to the nearest OI wall predicted the next 30 minutes — every bucket came out negative because those sessions were falling. Read the wall as *where the fuel is if a break comes*, never as a forecast that one will. See also [Call writers at round strikes](#) and [Make-or-break zone](#).

### Implied volatility (IV) regimes
- 12–13 is normal, 15–16 is costly, 27+ (like 11-Sep) is abnormal: premiums are very expensive and swings are violent.
- High IV helps near-ATM/ITM buyers while the move lasts, but premiums melt when volatility fades, especially before a holiday or expiry and in the second half.

### Designing a trade instead of predicting it
Take a defined risk, then shrink it step by step as price moves your way (trail, part-book, breakeven). Once the risk is neutral you don't need to be right about the target. This is the core of his psychology.
**Multi-lot ladder (7-Sep):** book ~1 lot at 1–1.5× the initial risk, move the rest to breakeven, then trail under each fresh higher low. With a single lot you can't ladder, so it's full target or full stop.
**Distribution zones as booking points (23-Sep):** on the 74900 CE he told viewers to move the stop up a ladder (252, then 269, then 277) and part-book each time price tagged a marked distribution zone, rather than waiting for one fixed target. The zones decide where to book; the trail decides when you're out.

### Jodi (long straddle)

### ITM / ATM / OTM basics
**What:** for a call, strikes below the current spot price are **in-the-money (ITM)**; above spot they're **out-of-the-money (OTM)**. For a put, it's the reverse: strikes above spot are ITM, below spot are OTM. The strike matching spot is **at-the-money (ATM)**.

### What "liquidity" means (plain definition)
**What:** liquidity is simply resting buy and sell orders sitting in the market. They cluster at swing highs/lows, order blocks, fair-value gaps, equal highs/lows, and prior day/week/month highs/lows — all the same zones used elsewhere in the playbook for stops and targets.
**How it connects:** this is the plainer, standalone version of the mechanism behind the "Liquidity sweep" and "Order flow" entries below — those describe what happens *to* this resting liquidity when big players hunt it.
**Watch:** [▶ 16-Sep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MK1NfHWN24M&t=21840s)

### Order flow: why limit orders beat chasing
Large players build positions quietly with **limit** orders resting just below obvious lows (and above obvious highs), exactly where retail stop losses sit. Retail chases with market orders and gets run over when those limits fill. This is the mechanism behind the liquidity sweep.

### Gamma blasts vs volatility spikes
Two different reasons a premium can suddenly jump:
- **Gamma:** the index itself moves fast, so the option's delta changes fast. It's most violent near expiry and in cheap OTM strikes, where the other Greeks are already thin. That's why expiry-day spikes feel so extreme.
- **Volatility ("wall") spike:** premium jumps because IV expands, with no matching move in the index. Sellers trade into these spikes.
His advice for retail: don't chase either one; stick to your setup.
The flip side (30-Sep): with neither a panic (IV flat) nor a big absolute index move (little delta), a premium just crawls even while the index drifts in your direction.
15-Oct (Sensex expiry) put numbers on it: with premiums cheap, 2–4-minute spikes of ₹10–40 are routine. **Disqualified when** the spike has no index momentum behind it ("hollow moves") — those give back as fast as they came. [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvf-FA_DAE0&t=13080s)

### Box days ("Test match" markets)
Some days price coils in a tight range for hours. He stops forcing trades and mentally treats the session like a slow Test match rather than a T20: a few small losses are the acceptable cost of not overtrading. ### Options drive the index
**What:** on a 1-minute chart it isn't the index moving the options but the options moving the index, because the option chart shows the buying and selling pressure first.
**Why it works:** option buyers and writers react before the cash index catches up; if writers are still defending a strike, the option will not confirm a spot move.
**Qualifies when:** the option breaks its own earlier high in the same direction as spot's move — that is when he trusts the move.
**Disqualified when:** spot and the option disagree. 14-Oct 11:24: Sensex made a fresh low but the 72800 PE had not broken its earlier high, so he read writers as still in control and did not buy the put [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSIAdI3_Gu8&t=8280s).
**Trigger:** not stated beyond the option's own break; he executes off the option chart and treats spot as context.
**Seen:** [▶ 23-Jul](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uu-LJo9Li30&t=21600s) · [▶ 14-Oct](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSIAdI3_Gu8&t=8280s)

### Trapping via a double top
If a second high forms *lower* than the first before a level breaks, more buyers get trapped at that second high. In his words, the bigger the trapping, the stronger the move the other way, which is why failed double tops often fuel sharp reversals.

### Don't carry your whole account overnight (BTST sizing)

### Don't read the first candle across brokers
Every broker captures ticks at slightly different moments, so the session's very first candle (its open, high and low) never matches exactly between charts, even though later candles line up. Don't build a level or a signal from a day-open wick you saw on one app.

### Pay a bit more for a liquid strike near expiry

### Near expiry, most premium is air
His analogy is a packet of chips: most of what's inside is air. Near expiry only a few strikes hold real (intrinsic) value; the rest of the premium is time value that disappears as the day goes on. That's why he avoids cheap out-of-the-money options on expiry day even when they look tempting.

### Absorption zone
A range where price is soaking up all the supply (or demand) before the next leg. Unlike a barcode, it isn't a coiled spring you trade the break of; it's simply a zone he refuses to trade inside, because moves there go nowhere.

### Reading OI as a change, not a number
A single OI snapshot tells you little. Look at how OI shifted over the last ~30 minutes around a consolidation: writers adding at a strike means they're defending it, and writers covering means they're giving up. Intraday OI confirms; it doesn't predict.

### Short covering, two kinds
A bounce after a big move can come from two different sources of buying: stop-loss-driven covering (shorts getting stopped out) or profit-booking-driven covering (shorts locking in gains). Both produce a similar-looking small bounce, which is why one reversal-looking candle isn't automatically a trend change — you can't tell which kind of covering it is just from the candle.

### Stop size comes from structure, not a % of premium
**What:** the stop goes where the price action is proven wrong, and he works out that stop *before* entering — if it is too large, he waits for the structure to give a nearer one instead of widening or skipping the rule.
**Why it works:** on a fast expiry-day run the last candle's low is far from price, so buying the running candle means risking 25–30 points for 30–60 of reward. A two- or three-candle pause builds a small base right under price, and the stop can sit under that instead. The quantity is then sized to the stop, never the other way round.
**Qualifies when:** the structural stop is about 10–15 points on a ~₹150 option (13-Oct: 159.79 / 144.47; 141.64 / 132.65), or about 20–25 on a ~₹235 Sensex option (14-Oct: 235.22 / 210.19), and the next zone pays at least about 2R. 30-Sep: he rejected a fixed-percentage stop outright — a ₹300 strike does not mean a ₹30 risk.
**Disqualified when:** the stop the entry needs is 25–28 points (13-Oct 09:26: a 157 entry meant a 127 stop, "big risk"); the stop is 20–22 points against 40 of reward ("1:1.7, not even 1:2", 13-Oct 11:26); or the stop is too large a share of the premium (29-Sep: ₹15 on a ₹100 strike). ### Max-pain drag on expiry morning
**What:** on expiry day, while the index sits near the max-pain area (the strike where option writers lose least), premiums on both sides bleed without moving, which favours sellers.
**Why it works:** writers defend the area because it is where their book pays most; a buyer there pays time decay for nothing.
**Qualifies when (to wait):** price lingers around the max-pain area for a long time (22-Oct: 250–300 on the 73100 options).
**Disqualified when:** price leaves the area and tags a clear zone on the option chart, with a sweep.
**Trigger:** none of its own; it is a reason to wait for the zone touch rather than buy the drift.

### The last bull becomes the bear (capitulation)
The worse the news gets *after* a long fall, the closer the market usually is to a real bottom — because bad news arriving late is what finally breaks the holdouts who have been buying all the way down. ### Jodi layering (Polymarket/Kalshi analogy)
When several jodis (long straddles) are open at once, he books each side in stages as it moves favourably rather than exiting the whole position at once — comparing it to toggling between "yes" and "no" positions on a prediction market. ### Enter above the higher high to shrink the stop
**What:** when a breakout candle shows rejection at its top, he does not buy it — he waits for price to take out its high, so the stop can sit under the last small candle instead of under the whole structure.
**Why it works:** the rejection wick is where the first breakout buyers were sold to. A trade above that high means the sellers who leaned on the wick are now offside, and the stop shrinks from the structural one to the last candle's low — the same idea at half the risk.
**Qualifies when:** a consolidation has broken, small pin bars or dojis form just under the level, and the structural stop is too big. On 9-Oct (22350 CE) that was ~20 points against a 226 target, about 1:1.5; above 199 it became ~9 points (191.15) for the same target.
**Disqualified when:** the stop is still ~20 points at the trigger ("I do not understand a 20-point risk"), or a trap forms instead of a higher high.
**Trigger:** price trades through the named higher high (199). Journal: 199.97 → 191.15 → 225.84, booked 220.14, +20.17.
**Seen:** [▶ 09:41 "only if the higher high forms — 199"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kn5B0LK1YM&t=1860s) · [▶ 09:43 refuses the 20-pt risk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kn5B0LK1YM&t=1980s) · [▶ 09:44 the 10-pt version](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kn5B0LK1YM&t=2040s)

### Skip the higher-high chase; wait for the pullback to the broken level
**What:** after price breaks to a new high, he does not buy the higher high; he waits for it to come back to the level it broke.
**Why it works:** the break is the crowded trade. A strong pullback right after a higher high takes the stops of everyone who bought it, and the move then continues without them; on 19-Oct a viewer who bought at 195 with no stop "became liquidity".
**Qualifies when:** he can name the pullback level (195 on the 23100 PE; 127–128 on the 22750 CE) and put a one-candle stop under it.
**Disqualified when:** price never comes back (he accepts missing it: "if it runs before, nothing to do"), the stop would be large, or the zone has already been touched twice. 27-Oct gave the reason in one line: "if the pullback doesn't come and you take the higher high, your stop is far, so the setup has no validity" — on the 22900 PE at 10:19 he preferred a dip to ~182 that never came [▶ 10:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=az8Ezg6JWks&t=4800s).
**Trigger:** the first green candle or pin bar at the pullback level, then above its high.
**Tension with "Enter above the higher high":** that concept takes the higher high when a rejection wick makes the stop small; this one refuses it when the stop is not. The plotted higher-high put he offered anyway at 12:55 (above 200, SL 183) lost −17.33 on a reversal wick.

### Who is trapped in a box (Trade Circuit)
**What:** when a 15-minute candle sweeps a low and closes back up, the buyers who bought that reclaim become the trapped side if hard selling follows; inside a sideways box, both sides keep getting trapped.
**Why it works:** the reclaim buyers' stops sit under the swept low; a fall back through it forces them to sell into the move. In a box every break pulls in breakout traders on one side and then returns.

**Disqualified when:** price is just inside a box with no displacement — "a sideways market gives entries on both sides and chases you both ways". Pure range days belong to those selling the range high and buying the range low.

### Pick the leg with the better reward-to-risk
**What:** at any moment both the call and the put have a pivot (27-Oct: CE 170, PE 168); he watches the leg holding above its pivot and trades the other only once the first loses its own.
**Why it works:** when both legs sit above their pivots the market is dull and premium decays on both; a clean close of one leg under its pivot hands the move to the other, and the leg whose next zone is further away pays more for the same stop.
**Qualifies when:** one leg closes under its pivot and the other prints a good candle, and the chosen leg's reward is better ("I'm more interested in the put because its RR is better").
**Disqualified when:** both legs are above their pivots, momentum is dull, or the chosen leg only offers 1:1.
**Trigger:** the close under the other leg's pivot, then a good candle on the favoured leg — the 22900 PE above 151 at 10:57 reached its 168 target (frame 11:25).

### Double bottom: wait for the second leg's panic
**What:** when a support breaks and price sits just under it, he does not buy the first bottom or the trend-line break; he waits for a second leg down that panics people out, then a strong candle.
**Why it works:** the second leg is where the late longs' stops sit; once they are flushed, the sellers who are left are few, and the next strong candle has less in its way.
**Qualifies when:** a previous support has broken and price is holding just below it in a double-bottom shape (29-Oct, Sensex options, 12:12–12:14).
**Disqualified when:** the only evidence is a trend-line breakout — "trend-line breakouts often give the entry at the bottom; I want more confirmations" — or no panic candle has printed yet.
**Trigger:** not stated beyond "a panic candle, then a strong candle". Treat this as a stated preference, not a rule with a measured record.
